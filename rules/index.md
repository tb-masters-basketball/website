# League Rules

*Draft Playing Rules*

## 1. Governing Rules

The Boston Pizza Masters Basketball League (BPMBL) shall be governed by the current FIBA Official Basketball Rules, except where specifically modified by these League Rules.

Where there is a conflict between FIBA rules and BPMBL rules, the BPMBL rule shall apply.

The intent of these modifications is to provide competitive basketball while recognizing the recreational and Masters nature of the League, available gym time, player safety and sportsmanship.

## 2. The Game

### 2.1 Teams

Basketball shall be played 5-on-5.

A team may begin a game with a minimum of four players.

If a team cannot field four eligible players within 10 minutes of the scheduled starting time, the game shall be recorded as a forfeit.

If a team is reduced below four players because of injury or fouls after the game begins, the officials may permit the game to continue provided the team has at least two players capable of playing.

### 2.2 Game Ball

A regulation Size 7 basketball shall be used. (Wilson Evolution)

Teams will provide a ball that the game - officials will confirm.

## 3. Game Length

### 3.1 Regulation Game

Games shall consist of:

- Four 10-minute quarters
- 2-minute interval between the first and second quarters
- 5-minute halftime
- 2-minute interval between the third and fourth quarters

**League Modification:** The use running time for portions of the game to ensure games remain within the scheduled gym time is at the discretion of the captains. (games must end on time)

Running time will be used except for:

- Timeouts
- Injuries
- Technical issues
- Situations where the referee specifically stops the clock

## 4. Overtime

If the game is tied following regulation:

- One 3-minute overtime period shall be played.
- Each team receives one timeout.
- Team fouls carry forward from the fourth quarter.

If the score remains tied following overtime:

**Regular Season:** Game may be recorded as a tie.

**Playoffs:** Additional 3-minute overtime periods shall be played until a winner is determined.

## 5. Shot Clock

Where a functioning shot clock and qualified operator are available, the FIBA 30-second shot clock shall be used.

FIBA shot-clock rules, including the 14-second reset, shall apply.

## 6. Backcourt

Normal FIBA backcourt rules apply.

A team gaining control of the ball in its backcourt must advance the ball into the frontcourt within 8 seconds.

Once frontcourt status has been established, the ball may not illegally be returned to the backcourt.

## 7. Three-Second Rule

Normal FIBA three-second rules apply.

An offensive player may not remain in the restricted area for more than three consecutive seconds while their team controls a live ball in the frontcourt and the game clock is running.

Officials should apply the rule according to normal FIBA principles and advantage/disadvantage.

## 8. Substitutions

Players must report to the scorer's table and may enter the game only when permitted by an official.

Substitutions may occur during any normal FIBA substitution opportunity.

There are no mandatory playing-time requirements.

## 9. Timeouts

Each team shall receive:

- **Two timeouts during the first half**
- **Two timeouts during the second half**
- **One timeout during each overtime period**

Unused timeouts do not carry into overtime.

Timeouts shall be 60 seconds.

FIBA procedures concerning when a timeout may be granted shall otherwise apply.

## 10. Personal Fouls

A player shall be disqualified from the game after committing five personal fouls.

Personal, unsportsmanlike and technical fouls count toward disqualification in accordance with FIBA rules.

## 11. Team Fouls / Bonus

Team fouls shall be counted by quarter.

Beginning with the fifth team foul of a quarter, subsequent defensive personal fouls shall result in the appropriate FIBA team-foul penalty.

Team fouls reset at the beginning of each quarter.

Fourth-quarter team fouls carry into overtime.

## 12. Free Throws

Players may enter the lane once the shot has clearly left the shooters hand.

The shooter must remain behind the free throw line until the ball has made contact.

One free throw is awarded for shooting fouls. The free throw will be worth what a successful attempt would have been worth. A foul shot after a made basket will still be worth one point as will bonus and penalty free throws.

## 13. Three-Point Field Goals

A successful field goal from beyond the court's designated three-point line shall count as three points.

Where a gym has multiple three-point lines, the officials shall identify the line being used before the game.

The League should use the FIBA three-point line wherever the facility provides one. (22.15ft top and wings to a minimum of 21.65ft along the baseline)

## 14. Defence

Although in recreational leagues man-to-man defence is encouraged, both man-to-man and zone defence are permitted.

Double teams, trapping and other conventional defensive strategies are permitted.

### Masters Mercy Rule

A team leading by 20 or more points shall not employ a full-court press.

The leading team must permit the opponent to advance the ball into the frontcourt before initiating extended defensive pressure.

Once the margin falls below 20 points, normal defensive rules resume.

Should the captains agree, run time can be used to accelerate a finish based on a lop-sided score or a team that is done getting its butt kicked.

## 15. Dunking

Dunking is permitted during games were permitted by the facility.

Players shall not deliberately hang on the rim except where necessary to avoid injury to themselves, another player or for a cool photo op.

Dunking during warmups shall be subject to facility rules.

## 16. Technical Fouls

Technical fouls shall generally be administered according to FIBA rules.

Technical fouls may be assessed for:

- Disrespectfully addressing an official
- Excessive arguing
- Taunting
- Profanity directed at another person
- Unsportsmanlike gestures
- Deliberately delaying the game
- Leaving the bench to participate in an altercation
- Other conduct deemed unsportsmanlike by the officials

Players are expected to communicate respectfully with officials.

Captains should be the primary point of communication with officials regarding significant questions or concerns.

Player code of conduct outlines these details specifically.

## 17. Unsportsmanlike Fouls

Officials may assess an unsportsmanlike foul where contact is:

- Excessive or unnecessary
- Not a legitimate attempt to play the basketball
- Intended primarily to stop an opponent rather than make a basketball play
- Dangerous or reckless
- Considered inconsistent with the spirit of Masters basketball

## 18. Fighting and Serious Misconduct

Fighting shall not be tolerated.

Any player who fights, attempts to fight, threatens another participant or deliberately engages in dangerous conduct shall be immediately ejected.

The incident shall be reported to League administration and may result in additional suspension.

Players leaving the bench to participate in an altercation may also be ejected (peacekeeping efforts are encouraged).

The League reserves the right to impose suspensions beyond penalties assessed by the game officials.

## 19. Respect for Officials (both referees and scorekeepers)

**We will adhere to the spirit of a zero-tolerance policy without actually writing one. To put it plainly, if you are a jerk, you will be kicked out of the league.**

Players and teams are expected to respect game officials.

Officials' judgement calls are final.

Players may respectfully request clarification of a rule, but prolonged arguing, intimidation, abusive language or personal attacks directed toward officials will not be tolerated.

Team captains are expected to assist officials in controlling the conduct of their players.

## 20. Advantage / Disadvantage

Officials are encouraged to apply normal basketball officiating principles and avoid unnecessary interruption of play for incidental contact that does not create an advantage or disadvantage.

The Masters League is intended to be competitive basketball, but normal incidental physical contact is expected.

Player safety shall take precedence over allowing physical play to continue.

## 21. Intentional Fouling at the End of Games

Strategic fouling is recognized as a legitimate part of basketball.

However, players attempting to stop the clock must make a reasonable basketball play.

Excessive, dangerous or unnecessary contact may be assessed as an unsportsmanlike foul.

## 22. Blood / Injury

A player who is bleeding or has blood on their uniform must leave the game until the bleeding has stopped and the affected area has been appropriately covered.

An injured player may be substituted immediately.

Officials may stop play immediately where they believe an injury may be serious.

Player safety takes priority over possession or game flow.

## 23. Uniforms

Players shall wear:

- Matching or substantially matching team jerseys
- Clearly visible numbers
- Appropriate athletic footwear

Players on the same team may not intentionally use duplicate numbers.

Where teams have similar colours, the team designated by the League as the home team shall wear the lighter uniform.

Teams should have access to both light and dark jerseys or pinnies where possible.

All uniform changes (when you order new jerseys) are required a check in with the commissioner first.

## 24. Minimum Players and Forfeits

A team should have five players ready to play at the scheduled game time.

A game may proceed with four players.

A team unable to field four players after the League's grace period forfeits the game.

Recommended official forfeit score:

**Q1 1-0, Q2 1-0, Q3 1-0, Q4 1-0**

Teams are strongly encouraged to play an exhibition game where possible following a forfeit so players who attended still receive playing time.

## 25. Mercy / Running Clock

Where one team leads by 20 or more points during the fourth quarter, the clock may remain running with the agreement of both captains.

Normal stop-time shall resume if the margin falls below 20 points.

Officials may stop the clock for injuries, timeouts or other exceptional circumstances.

## 26. Possession Arrow

The game shall begin with a jump ball.

All subsequent held-ball situations shall be determined using the alternating possession arrow in accordance with FIBA rules.

Overtime periods begin in accordance with FIBA rules.

## 27. Officials

Game officials have authority over the game from their arrival on the court until completion of their post-game responsibilities.

Judgement calls made by officials are not subject to protest.

Video replay shall not be used to overturn routine officiating decisions.

## 28. Masters Basketball Principle

The BPMBL recognizes that players remain competitive regardless of age.

The League therefore encourages officials to allow normal basketball contact while protecting players from unnecessary or dangerous contact.

Attention should be given to:

- Contact with airborne players
- Under-cutting shooters
- Excessive contact on fast breaks
- Dangerous screens
- Reckless contact around the basket
- Retaliatory fouls
- Unnecessary contact away from the ball

The expectation is simple:

**Play hard. Play basketball. Go to work Monday.**

## 29. Rules Not Specifically Covered

Any playing situation not specifically addressed by these League Rules shall be governed by the current FIBA Official Basketball Rules.

Where an unusual situation cannot be resolved through FIBA rules, the game officials shall make the decision necessary to continue the game and report the matter to Matt afterward.

League may subsequently issue a rule interpretation that shall apply to future games.

## 30. Standings

Masters League uses the old ABA league scoring system to rank teams.

1 point is awarded for each quarter won and 3 points is awarded to the winning team for a maximum total of 7 points. This is intended to keep games competitive by awarding points for each quarter of each game.

## 31. Schedule

Teams will play a balanced number of games each, currently this is at 16 games plus a playoff (three additional games max).

This year, to improve competitiveness, the mid point of the season (after 6 games) teams may be seeded and will play more games against teams one seed above and one seed below them. Taking games away from teams that are either easy wins or blowouts.

## 32. Eligible Players

Masters basketball is an open league, no rule is in place to define who can play other than age. Players must be 40 or turning 40 during the year they are playing. To help answer the first anticipated question, a player turning 40 after the Christmas break may not play in games prior to the Christmas break. Capiche?

# Player Code of Conduct

*No Jerks Allowed*

## 1. Purpose

The League exists to provide competitive, respectful, and safe basketball for adult players. Sportsmanship is expected at all times.

This code of practice is governed by the captains and the commissioner of the league, changes can be made at the discretion of that group.

## 2. Core Expectations (Players, Captains, Spectators)

**All participants must:**

- Treat opponents, teammates, officials, and facility staff with respect.
- Play hard but under control; avoid reckless or dangerous contact.
- Accept officials' decisions without prolonged argument.
- Use appropriate language (no abuse, threats, discriminatory remarks, or harassment).
- Keep the game moving (quick check-ins, minimal delays, no "extra" stoppages).
- Arrive ready to play and follow facility rules (shoes, cleanliness, no alcohol/drugs).

**Captains are responsible for:**

- Managing their bench and de-escalating disputes.
- Ensuring rosters are eligible and players understand the rules.
- Communicating with the League regarding discipline issues.

## 3. Sportsmanship and Conflict

- If a situation escalates, players are expected to step back immediately.
- Only the captain (or one designated player) may speak to officials about rule interpretation.
- "Play on" mentality is encouraged; retaliatory plays are not tolerated.

## Technical and Flagrant Fouls Framework

## 4. Technical Fouls (T)

A Technical is for unsporting behaviour, not basketball contact.

**Common reasons:**

- Persistent complaining, arguing, or disrespectful conduct toward officials
- Taunting, baiting, or excessive trash talk crossing into personal/abusive territory
- Profanity directed at others
- Delaying the game (refusing to check, slapping ball away, intentional stalling)
- Bench misconduct

**Penalty structure:**

- **T1 (in a game):** One technical foul; standard free throws/possession per your game rules and player out for remainder of quarter (regardless of time left).
- **T2 (same player, same game):** Automatic ejection.

**Season escalation:**

- **2 technicals in a season:** Automatic one-game suspension.
- **3 technicals in a season:** Next game suspension and first playoff game suspension
- **4 technicals in a season:** Same as 3 but with a second playoff game missed
- **5 technicals in a season:** Season over (if it's the last game of the year the player will miss the first two games of next season)

## 5. Flagrant Fouls (F1 / F2)

A Flagrant is for unnecessary and/or excessive contact or conduct that risks injury or escalates conflict.

### Flagrant 1 (F1) – Unnecessary / Dangerous

**Examples:**

- Hard foul with no play on the ball
- Contact to head/neck area
- Under-cutting a shooter or sliding under airborne player
- Excessive contact on a fast break from behind
- Reckless elbowing, swinging arms, "football tackle" style contact

**Penalty:**

- Free throws/possession per league rules.
- Tracked by the League.

### Flagrant 2 (F2) – Excessive / Violent / Fight-Adjacency

**Examples:**

- Punching, striking, or attempting to strike
- Deliberate elbow/forearm to head/neck
- Aggressive shove after the whistle
- Leaving the bench to join an altercation
- Any action judged to be fighting, intimidation, or retaliation

**Penalty:**

- Immediate ejection
- Automatic minimum suspension (recommend 2 games, more depending on severity)
- Mandatory discipline review; possible season removal

## 6. Zero-Tolerance Categories

**These trigger automatic ejection and league discipline review:**

- Fighting or attempted fighting
- Threats of violence
- Discriminatory/harassing language (race, religion, gender, sexuality, etc.)
- Spitting
- Contact with an official (intentional physical contact)

## 7. Ejections, Suspensions, and Discipline Process

**To keep it fair and consistent:**

1. Same-night: Ejected player leaves the playing area immediately.
1. League reviews scorebook notes + official report + captain input.
1. Decision: Suspension length communicated to captains.

**Recommended minimums:**

- **Flagrant 1:** Usually no suspension unless repeat behaviour or injury risk.
- **Flagrant 2:** Minimum 2-game suspension.
- **Fight:** Minimum 3-game suspension; repeat = season removal.

## 8. Forfeits and Game Control

**Officials (or league rep) may declare a forfeit if:**

- A team refuses to continue play
- A team cannot control its bench
- Player conduct creates an unsafe environment
- Multiple ejections prevent safe continuation
